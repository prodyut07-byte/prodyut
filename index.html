Research

agenda

My dissertation builds individual-level social networks between investment bankers and institutional fund managers in the U.S. equity IPO market, then tests whether those relationships improve market efficiency or worsen the agency problem that governs share allocation.

Job Market Paper

"Efficiency or Favoritism? Social Ties Between Underwriters and Institutional Investors in the IPO Market"

IPO shares are priced and allocated through bookbuilding, a process that runs on relationships and underwriter discretion — yet U.S. underwriters are not required to disclose allocations, and prior work has measured these relationships only at the institutional level. I construct director-level social networks using an AI agent to link three datasets that share no common identifier — SDC Platinum IPO data, 13-F institutional holdings, and BoardEx — and test four hypotheses spanning allocation, underpricing, aftermarket trading, and long-run performance to adjudicate between an efficiency view and a favoritism view of these ties.

Proposal defense: 21st September 2026. Dataset and network constructed — results not yet reported.

Working paper

"Unveiling Market Manipulation Dynamics in IPO Events and Lock-Up Expirations"

With Dr. Douglass Cumming. Investigates stock manipulation in non-IPO stocks during periods of heightened investor attention to newly public companies; finds increased manipulation activity during hot IPO listing periods.

Working paper

"The Influence of Venture Capital Exits on Investee Companies' Financial Health"

With Dr. Sofia Johan. Examines how voluntary and involuntary VC exits affect investee companies' subsequent performance, governance, and long-run growth.

Working paper

"Impact of the Panama Papers Leak on Venture Capital Funds"

With Dr. Sofia Johan. Using Pitchbook and the Corporate Tax Haven Index, shows the 2016 leak acted as a critical juncture that shifted VC investment toward more transparent, onshore-oriented strategies.

Next project

Triadic issuer–underwriter–investor networks

Extends this dissertation's network-construction methodology to a triadic network combining issuer–underwriter ties with underwriter–investor ties in a single framework.
